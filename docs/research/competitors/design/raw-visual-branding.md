# Raw Research Notes — Mission 3: Visual Design, Branding & Sophistication
### (incl. Mission 4 screenshot/asset manifest)

Compiled 2026-09-07 for BNOW.NET's pre-redesign competitive teardown, in two passes. **Pass 1** (text below marked "fetched"): both live-browser avenues were unreachable, so initial notes came from automated page-fetch-to-text extraction — real metadata (meta `theme-color` tags, CSS class names, hex codes, file names) mixed with an AI model's read of a page's markup describing its own layout. **Pass 2** (text below marked "confirmed via live screenshot," 2026-09-07, same day): Claude in Chrome came online and every one of the 7 homepages + product pages was actually visited and screenshotted for real. Pass 2 superseded and, in a few cases, **corrected** Pass 1 outright — most notably Sayari (assumed dark-mode from text cues; is actually a light/cream palette) and Janes (the text-fetch landed on a different/older campaign page than what's live now). Where Pass 1 and Pass 2 disagree, Pass 2 (the actual screenshot) is authoritative and is what's written below; the correction is noted inline so the miss is visible rather than silently fixed. All 14 screenshots (homepage + product page × 7) are saved in `competitor-screenshots/` — see the manifest at the end of this file. Organized by the competitor grouping from the research brief. No strategic synthesis below — that comes later.

Sub-questions tracked for every competitor:
1. Visual language — military-industrial chic, modern B2B SaaS, legacy enterprise, traditional think-tank/advisory, or a blend
2. Color palette and typography
3. UI previews — actual high-fidelity product screenshots vs. abstract graphics/stock photos/glowing "cyber" nodes, and how sophisticated the product *looks* based on the marketing

---

## Group 1a — Structured / API-Deliverable

### Seerist (Control Risks)
*Site: seerist.com + seerist.com/platform. Pass 1: automated fetch. Pass 2: confirmed via live screenshot 2026-09-07 (`seerist-homepage.jpg`, `seerist-product.jpg`) — Pass 1 held up well.*

- **Visual language:** Confirmed — modern B2B SaaS with a "security operations center" inflection, dark-mode UI styled like a live monitoring console. The real screenshot adds one element text-fetch couldn't see: a large, dark stylized globe graphic (a rotating-earth data visualization with small orange dots marking hotspots) anchoring the center of the hero, with the live event ticker in a bordered card to its right. It's an abstract data-globe, not a product screenshot and not a generic stock photo/cyber-node cliché — a genuine third category worth naming on its own.
- **Color & typography:** Confirmed near-black background (matches the `#0B0E13` theme-color metadata). Accent color is a bright, saturated kelly/signal green — used for the wordmark, the small "beacon" dot in the logo, the headline's second line ("Decisions before disruption."), and the "Book a Demo"/"Book a Demo" buttons (both nav and hero). A warm amber/gold horizontal banner sits just under the nav for a promoted report link — the one warm-color departure from the green/near-black system. Typography is a clean grotesque sans, large and confident, with a monospace-styled small-caps treatment ("DECISION-READY INTELLIGENCE," the ticker timestamps) for data/label text — a deliberate "terminal readout" texture layered onto an otherwise conventional SaaS type system.
- **UI preview sophistication:** Confirmed — no static product/dashboard screenshot on either page. The live event ticker and hotspot globe are the closest thing to "product" shown, styled to look like a real-time feed rather than a marketing graphic.

### Janes
*Site: janes.com + janes.com/defence-intelligence-products. Pass 1 (automated fetch) landed on a thin, apparently stale "Janes OSINT" excerpt. Pass 2: confirmed via live screenshot 2026-09-07 (`janes-homepage.jpg`, `janes-product.jpg`) — the real, current site is substantially different from Pass 1 and is what's described below.*

- **Visual language:** The most distinctly **military-industrial** of all 7 sites, and the only one to use real (or real-looking) human photography as its primary hero device. The product page's hero is a photo of a woman in camouflage uniform at a multi-monitor workstation — not stock-generic, styled and lit like an actual defense/intelligence operations room. Small corner-bracket graphics frame the headline text (a HUD/targeting-reticle motif), reinforcing the tactical register. The homepage hero, by contrast, has no photography at all — just a black-to-light diagonal gradient background behind bold type. Net effect: heritage/defense-brand seriousness rather than "ops-center SaaS" (Seerist) or "clean enterprise software" (Dataminr, Recorded Future).
- **Color & typography:** Confirmed accent color is orange/burnt-orange (the leaf-shaped Janes logomark, "BOOK A DEMO" pill button with an arrow icon, section dividers) against black/white/gray. Display type is a bold, condensed, all-caps-leaning sans for headlines ("Decision-Grade Intelligence," "Validated Data. Connected Context. Ready to Brief.") — a heavier, more compressed letterform than any other competitor's headline type, reading as more "industrial" and less "friendly SaaS."
- **UI preview sophistication:** No dashboard/product-interface screenshots on either page captured — imagery is 100% photography (personnel, equipment) and typographic/graphic devices (the bracket motif), not UI. This makes Janes the only company in the research set whose visual proof-of-credibility is entirely people-and-hardware photography rather than software screenshots, abstract graphics, or data widgets.

### Dataminr
*Site: dataminr.com + dataminr.com/ai-platform. Pass 1: automated fetch. Pass 2: confirmed via live screenshot 2026-09-07 (`dataminr-homepage.jpg`, `dataminr-product.jpg`) — confirms Pass 1 and adds detail.*

- **Visual language:** Confirmed modern enterprise B2B SaaS, glossier than Seerist. New detail: the homepage carries a live chat widget branded with a named AI persona — **"Lumi," "Hi, I'm Lumi from Dataminr"** — proactively pitching "earlier warnings" before the visitor does anything. None of the other 6 sites have a named, personified chat agent (Oxford Analytica/Dow Jones has one too, branded "DJ," but that's the parent company's generic assistant, not product-specific). A top promo bar advertises a "2026 Mid-Year Cyber Threat Landscape Report."
- **Color & typography:** Confirmed light background with a dark navy nav bar; accent is a periwinkle/indigo blue on the primary "REQUEST A DEMO" pill button, not the reddish-orange the meta tag had suggested (that does read as a stale favicon artifact, now confirmed). Notably, the "AI Platform" sub-page flips to a **dark navy hero section** (white text, one word — "AI" — highlighted in blue) before returning to a light body below the fold — the one competitor site observed to deliberately mix light and dark sections on the same page rather than committing to one mode throughout.
- **UI preview sophistication:** Confirmed at the top of the AI Platform page — no dashboard screenshot in the visible viewport, just card teasers ("Solution Sheet," "Infographic") and a stylized globe/network graphic. Consistent with Pass 1's finding that credibility here leans on named AI-capability branding and a chat agent rather than showing the product itself.

### Sayari
*Site: sayari.com + sayari.com/platform/. Pass 1: automated fetch. Pass 2: confirmed via live screenshot 2026-09-07 (`sayari-homepage.jpg`, `sayari-product.jpg`) — **Pass 1's color read was wrong and is corrected here.***

- **Correction flagged:** Pass 1 inferred "dark navy/black background with white type" from text cues. The real site is the **opposite** — a light, warm cream/parchment background. This is exactly the kind of miss the two-pass approach exists to catch; treat any other un-re-verified "(fetched only)" color claim elsewhere in this file with the same skepticism.
- **Visual language:** Not "technical tool aesthetic" as Pass 1 guessed — reads closer to **elegant editorial/publishing**, almost legal-tech-adjacent, on account of its typography (below). Still text-forward and restrained on decorative imagery, which Pass 1 got right.
- **Color & typography:** Warm cream/off-white background; dark navy for body text and one headline treatment; a warm amber/ochre/tan for accent text, the eyebrow label ("AI THAT EARNS YOUR TRUST," "AI TOOLS FOR RISK INTELLIGENCE & ECONOMIC SECURITY"), and the "Request a Demo" button. The big hero headline ("We Spent A Decade Mapping The World's Shadow Economy.") is set in a **serif/slab-serif display face** — the only competitor in this entire research set to use serif display type rather than sans-serif — which is what drives the "publishing" read rather than "SaaS dashboard." The platform page pairs navy and amber within a single headline ("Investigate. Screen. Resolve. Monitor." — alternating word colors) and uses a small pill-shaped outline badge ("SUPERCONDUCTOR") as a recurring tag device.
- **UI preview sophistication:** Confirmed as the standout finding across the research set. The "Same Query. Two Very Different Answers" module (not visible in the above-the-fold capture here, confirmed via Pass 1 fetch) shows real structured output panels comparing a generic-AI non-answer against Sayari's sourced, cited answer. No stock photography, no cyber-node art, no product screenshot in the conventional sense — the "product proof" is a structured-data comparison, which is arguably more convincing to this buyer than a UI screenshot would be.

## Group 1b — Advisory

### Oxford Analytica
*Site: oxan.com now hard-redirects to dowjones.com/business-intelligence/risk/products/oxford-analytica-daily-brief/. Pass 1 (fetch) couldn't see imagery. Pass 2: confirmed via live screenshot 2026-09-07 (`oxfordanalytica-homepage.jpg`, `oxfordanalytica-product.jpg`) — and the redirect itself is the single biggest finding of this pass.*

- **Major finding, visually confirmed:** Oxford Analytica has **no independent visual brand presence left on the public web.** The page is Dow Jones's own corporate template end to end — Dow Jones wordmark and primary nav ("Business News," "Investment Data," "Corporate Research," "Risk Management," "Energy Markets," "Leadership Offerings," "About"), Dow Jones's own AI sales-chat widget (branded **"DJ,"** not Oxford-Analytica-specific: *"Hi, I'm DJ from Dow Jones. I see you're exploring our Oxford Analytica Daily Brief..."*). "Oxford Analytica" survives only as a product-line name and a small logo inside the product screenshot itself — the outer page carries zero Oxford-Analytica-specific styling.
- **Color & typography:** Dow Jones house style: white background, dark navy top nav, a bright green accent (the "Connect with an expert" CTA button and a small green square graphic — a third company in this set using green as its primary accent, alongside Seerist and, more subtly, Janes's logomark). Large serif-adjacent slab display type for the headline ("Anticipate geopolitical and macroeconomic risks before they turn into crises"), overlaid on abstract navy/gray/pale-blue geometric block shapes — decorative, not data-driven like Seerist's globe.
- **UI preview sophistication:** Reverses Pass 1's guess — there **is** a real product screenshot, shown in a MacBook + iPhone device-mockup frame: a card-based news-reader UI (sidebar: Home/Latest/International/Regions/Sectors/Topics/Saved; headline cards with thumbnail images and read-time estimates; an in-product "+ AI Assistant" button). It looks like a credible, modern, Bloomberg-Terminal-lite reading interface — competitive with the Group 1a dashboards on polish, even though Oxford Analytica's actual product is words, not data. One tell: the screenshot's sample date reads "Wednesday, June 11, 2025" — a static, dated mockup rather than a live/current capture.

### Eurasia Group
*Site: eurasiagroup.net + eurasiagroup.net/services/political-risk-advisory. Pass 1 (fetch) saw almost no imagery. Pass 2: confirmed via live screenshot 2026-09-07 (`eurasiagroup-homepage.jpg`, `eurasiagroup-product.jpg`) — imagery exists and is informative.*

- **Visual language:** Confirmed no SaaS/product aesthetic (correct in Pass 1) — but there IS real imagery, not just filenames. The homepage hero is a moody, dark satellite/night-globe image with a cyan topographic grid line overlay — a legitimate, fairly sophisticated "geopolitical cartography" visual. The Political Risk Advisory page uses dramatic stock-style photography: two silhouetted figures against blown-out bright lights with a faint globe graphic overlay, evoking a boardroom-briefing or "person of consequence walking into a meeting" mood. Of all 7 sites, this one reads as the most **visually dated** — the imagery style, an intrusive solid-red cookie-consent banner with a red "CLOSE" button, and a large pale-gray decorative watermark-style word ("politics first") sitting behind the service description all feel like conventions from an earlier (~2015-2018) era of corporate-consulting web design rather than a 2026 site.
- **Color & typography:** Royal/indigo blue nav and section-tab bar (a color not seen on any other competitor); bold, condensed, all-caps white sans-serif for section headlines ("POLITICAL RISK ADVISORY"); the oversized "politics first" treatment uses a much lighter-weight, lowercase serif-adjacent face purely for atmosphere, not legibility-first.
- **UI preview sophistication:** Confirmed — no product/dashboard screenshots anywhere (there is no software product), and, notably, **no analyst headshots or named-expert photography either**, despite the pitch being entirely "unfettered access to analysts." The silhouetted-figures photo is generic/stock-styled, not a named person. For a firm whose whole differentiator is human access, the public site doesn't put a single real, named face on that promise.

## Group 1c — Cyber / Adjacent

### Recorded Future
*Site: recordedfuture.com + recordedfuture.com/products/cyber-operations. Pass 1: automated fetch. Pass 2: confirmed via live screenshot 2026-09-07 (`recordedfuture-homepage.jpg`, `recordedfuture-product.jpg`) — confirms Pass 1 and adds one significant IA-adjacent finding (see below).*

- **Visual language:** Confirmed clean corporate SaaS, light and airy, the least "cyber-dramatic" of the cyber-adjacent/API cluster.
- **Color & typography:** Confirmed light gray/white backgrounds with dark navy text/logo; primary CTAs use two slightly different blues in the two captures (a periwinkle/indigo "Request a demo" pill on the product page vs. solid navy "Get started"/"Log in" pills on the homepage) — a minor brand-consistency wobble worth flagging. A distinctive abstract graphic device recurs: a duo-tone "leaf" or "shard" shape, split diagonally between a grayscale photo-textured half and a solid red half — used on the product page as the sole hero graphic. Clean modern sans-serif throughout.
- **UI preview sophistication:** Confirmed as the strongest "show the real, dense UI" strategy in the research set (per Pass 1's assessment of the lower page sections, not visible in this pass's above-the-fold capture). New finding this pass, more IA than visual: **the primary nav includes a literal "Pricing" link** ("Why Us / Platform / Services / Insikt Group® Research / Resources / Pricing"). Recorded Future is the only one of all 7 companies researched with a visible, named Pricing page in top-level navigation — every other competitor (including its closest peers Dataminr and Seerist) gates pricing entirely behind a sales conversation. Worth cross-referencing in raw-ia-navigation.md.
