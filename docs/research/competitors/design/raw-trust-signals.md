# Raw Research Notes — Mission 2: Trust Signals & Psychological Belonging

Compiled 2026-09-07 for BNOW.NET's pre-redesign competitive teardown. Unsynthesized field notes on how each competitor establishes authority with defense/IC/enterprise-risk buyers. Organized by the competitor grouping from the research brief. Quoted text is verbatim from the live site; paraphrase is marked as such. **Methodology note:** built from automated page-fetch-to-markdown extraction (as with the IA-navigation file); two items below (Dataminr's and Oxford Analytica's AI chat widgets) were spotted only once live screenshots became available mid-session and are flagged inline as Pass 2 additions rather than corrections. No strategic synthesis below — that comes later.

Sub-questions tracked for every competitor:
1. Trust signals used — government seals, compliance badges (FedRAMP, SOC 2, ISO), partner/customer logos, named methodology whitepapers, advisory board / analyst bios, press mentions, awards
2. How "tradecraft" or methodology is communicated, if at all — source grading, confidence levels, named standards (ICD/IC tradecraft, admiralty code equivalents), analyst credentials, transparency about process
3. The psychological pitch — exclusive access, AI speed, military-grade security, bespoke human expertise, some blend, or something else entirely

---

## Group 1a — Structured / API-Deliverable

### Seerist (Control Risks)
*Site: seerist.com + seerist.com/platform (fetched 2026-09-07)*

- **Trust signals:** Unusually dense logo wall for this category — 13 named organizations including **NATO**, **ODNI**, **US Intelligence Community**, **Department of War**, plus commercial names (Diageo, Takeda, Telenor, Wärtsilä, Kawasaki, American Axle, Aker Security, Solar Turbines, Norwich University). Government/institutional seals used as literal crest imagery, not just wordmarks. Quantified social proof: "Trusted by 400+ organizations worldwide," "25% of the Fortune 100." One named, attributed customer quote (Nick Sivyer, Global Security Operations Center Lead, American Axle & Manufacturing) — a named human + title + company, not an anonymous pull-quote. Notably, the deeper Platform/product page carries **none** of these trust signals — all social proof is front-loaded onto the homepage only.
- **Tradecraft / methodology communication:** No named IC standard (no ICD/ICS-style citation), no published methodology whitepaper surfaced in this pass, no visible source-reliability grading scale. The language of rigor is "verification" as a feature/brand word: "Verified Events add human confirmation, source validation, and event-level context," "80% of events verified," "Every answer cited, sourced, and traceable," "50 years of HUMINT depth." Trust is pitched via human pedigree + verification-as-feature-name, not a disclosed/auditable methodology document.
- **Psychological pitch:** A fusion pitch — (a) bespoke human expertise ("200+ Control Risks analysts, embedded," "50 years of HUMINT depth"), (b) AI-scaled speed ("50,000 analyst hours, queryable in 30 seconds," conversational AI copilot "AskAnna"), (c) institutional/exclusive validation (NATO, ODNI, US IC, Dept. of War logos), (d) urgency/relief framing ("3AM call," "I have 90 minutes," a "hidden cost of intelligence work" chart showing analysts burn 40% of time on collection alone). Explicitly sells the combination — "speed and trust" — not one lever alone.

### Janes
*Site: janes.com (fetched 2026-09-07; thin capture, see IA notes)*

- **Trust signals:** None visible in the captured excerpt (no logos/certifications/case studies surfaced) — likely present elsewhere on the full site but not in this fetch pass. The one quantified proof point offered is internal: "500,000+ Analyst hours per year."
- **Tradecraft/methodology:** The standout line is explicitly **anti-AI-filter**: "Janes data is verified and validated by our teams of analysts, not AI filters" — a direct, pointed contrast to the AI-forward pitches of Dataminr, Sayari, and (partly) Seerist. No named IC standard or published methodology surfaced this pass.
- **Psychological pitch:** Heritage/authority ("the trusted source," implied brand legacy) + human-verification-over-AI + comprehensiveness ("one source of truth" framing, large data-scale numbers).

### Dataminr
*Site: dataminr.com + dataminr.com/ai-platform (fetched 2026-09-07)*

- **Trust signals:** Heavy on scale and government logos rather than named individuals — "two-thirds of Fortune 50," "half of Fortune 100," "100+ U.S. government agencies," "20+ international governments." Named institutional logos: U.S. Department of Defense, U.S. Department of State, CISA, NATO. Commercial namedrops: Goldman Sachs, Microsoft, NVIDIA, OpenAI, Walmart. One specific, press-covered contract: "$318 Million Contract with the U.S. Department of War" (First Alert Advanced). Awards: Fortune Future 50, Frost & Sullivan leadership recognition. One named customer quote (Danske Bank's Chief Threat and Risk Analyst). No advisory board or analyst bios found.
- **Tradecraft/methodology:** Essentially none in IC-tradecraft terms — the "rigor" story is entirely AI-architecture ("Multi-Modal Fusion AI → Generative AI → Agentic AI," "50+ proprietary LLMs," "99.5% accuracy" for auto-regenerating briefs) rather than human sourcing/verification standards. No confidence-level taxonomy, no source-reliability grading, no named intelligence standard anywhere observed.
- **Psychological pitch:** Speed/first-mover ("earliest warnings," "know first, act faster"), scale-as-authority ("1M data sources," "50+ proprietary LLMs"), government/defense heritage (the $318M DoW contract does a lot of trust work), protection framing ("protect what matters most"). Reads as the most purely "AI speed" pitch of the group so far — noticeably light on human-analyst-in-the-loop language versus Seerist, Janes, or Sayari.
- **Pass 2 addition (live screenshot, not visible to the text fetch):** a branded AI chat widget, **"Lumi,"** sits fixed in the bottom-right corner of both the homepage and the AI Platform page — a persistent, always-available conversational-AI affordance baked into the site chrome itself, not just described in marketing copy. Reinforces the "AI speed" pitch above at the interaction-design level, not just the copy level: the site itself demonstrates the AI-forward positioning rather than only claiming it.

### Sayari
*Site: sayari.com (fetched 2026-09-07)*

- **Trust signals:** The richest and most specific set gathered so far. Customer logos: BNP Paribas, BP, ExxonMobil, HMRC, HSBC, Microsoft, Tesla, plus a U.S. Government seal. Compliance: **SOC 2 Type II** — the first formal compliance certification observed in this research pass. Investor roster used openly as a trust signal: Centana, **In-Q-Tel (named explicitly as "the CIA's venture arm")**, LavRock, Mission OG, TPG Capital. Named press coverage: WSJ ("$228M from TPG"), Financial Times (Orbán-oligarchs investigation), PYMNTS (In-Q-Tel investment). Named government contracts: "$8.5M contract with U.S. Treasury Department," "$7.8M Enterprise Trade Analytics Contract" (CBP). 2025 awards: Inc. 5000 (4th consecutive year), Deloitte Fast 500, SupplyTech Breakthrough Award. Four detailed-but-anonymized case studies ("No. 3 Global Telecom Provider," "Fortune 100 Industrial Manufacturer," etc.) with quantified outcomes.
- **Tradecraft/methodology:** The deepest explicit tradecraft language found in Group 1a. Direct quotes: "trained on the reasoning skills drawn from thousands of real investigations," "Tradecraft standards before it reaches your team," "Every output is scored, sourced, and explainable," "When the AI doesn't know something, it says so." Most notably, Sayari uses **probabilistic confidence language that reads as IC-tradecraft-adjacent**: "Calibrated Uncertainty," "Likely exposure: Roughly Even Chance to Likely" — structurally similar to Words of Estimative Probability as used in ICD 203-style analytic standards, though Sayari does not name ICD 203 or any specific IC issuance. Framed as a direct rebuttal to generic LLMs: "No chain-of-ownership traversal · No source citation · Unverifiable" (generic AI) vs. "Grounded. Specific. Calibrated uncertainty" (Sayari).
- **Psychological pitch:** "Trustworthy/explainable AI" as the core wedge against both generic LLMs and (implicitly) less-transparent competitors — "The gap isn't data. It isn't AI. It's the judgment between them." Layers in government-grade credibility (In-Q-Tel/CIA, Treasury, CBP), an ~11-year heritage claim, and speed-at-scale ("sub-second latency"). Explicitly targets buyers with personal accountability exposure (compliance/procurement/legal) via loss-aversion language ("Audit failure, penalty, loss of trust").

## Group 1b — Advisory

### Oxford Analytica
*Site: oxan.com (fetched 2026-09-07)*

- **Trust signals:** Named blue-chip/institutional clients — **The World Bank, European Commission, Norwegian Red Cross, Gates Foundation, Fortune 50 companies, G7 governments**, a named Canadian government department, an EU institution. Survey-based social proof: "94% of recent survey respondents rated Daily Brief analysis as 'important' to their work" (Dec 2022–Jan 2023 — a dated, specific survey rather than a vague claim). Two named client-side testimonials (Kyle Lawless, EY; Sam Wilkin, WTW) — notably these are testimonials *from* people at EY/WTW, not employees. Longevity claim: founded 1975, "nearly 50 years." Separately, a GSA Advantage listing was found via search, indicating Oxford Analytica sits on a U.S. federal (GSA) procurement schedule — a real but not homepage-advertised government-credibility signal.
- **Tradecraft/methodology:** Thin and generic relative to its claims — "robust methodologies and impartial analysis," "rigorous process ensures analysis remains independent and unbiased," analysis built by "a unique collaboration of in-house experts and a 1,500-strong global network." No named framework, no confidence-level taxonomy, no source-reliability grading, no citation standard disclosed anywhere found. **Notable, somewhat counterintuitive finding:** the dedicated "Advisory" people/leadership page does NOT sell named-expert credibility — it lists six people by internal title only (Director & Head of Oxford Analytica Europe, etc.) with **no former-government, ambassadorial, or academic affiliations mentioned**, and explicitly frames itself around generic institutional capacity ("Make better decisions with Oxford Analytica") rather than individual pedigree. For a firm whose entire category pitch is "bespoke human expertise," the public bios are thin.
- **Psychological pitch:** Anxiety/FOMO-inflected ("Your passport to global risk mitigation," "navigate the complexities," implicit 24-hour-news-cycle urgency) combined with legacy authority (50-year track record) and a "designed for busy professionals" efficiency angle — a curated daily brief that *replaces* scattered research, rather than augmenting a research team the way the API tools pitch themselves.
- **Pass 2 addition (live screenshot, not visible to the text fetch) — echoes the Recorded Future/Mastercard pattern:** the product page carries a branded AI chat widget labeled **"DJ"** — a strong, unadvertised signal that Oxford Analytica sits inside the **Dow Jones** corporate family (Dow Jones being a well-known owner of business/financial-intelligence media properties). As with Recorded Future's unadvertised Mastercard ownership, this is a real corporate-backing trust signal — Dow Jones is a globally recognizable, credible name — that Oxford Analytica is not spending any homepage or product-page copy on. Worth flagging as a second instance of the same pattern: at least two of the seven competitors researched are owned by large, trusted commercial brands and neither one is using that ownership as public-facing trust collateral. Unconfirmed whether "DJ" definitively stands for Dow Jones vs. some other internal product name — flagged as a strong inference from the widget branding and initial, not a fact independently verified via corporate filings or an About page in this pass.

### Eurasia Group
*Site: eurasiagroup.net (fetched 2026-09-07)*

- **Trust signals:** Comparatively sparse on the pages fetched — no client logos, no compliance badges, no formal case studies. What trust signal exists is built almost entirely around one named individual, **Ian Bremmer** (referenced via his "2025 State of the World speech"), plus institutional weight from offices in New York, Washington, and London and "on-the-ground experts and resources in over ninety-five countries." Thought-leadership publications used as proof-of-expertise rather than client logos: "Top Risks 2026" (flagship annual report) and "Atlas of Impunity: Erosion of Press Freedom."
- **Tradecraft/methodology:** The thinnest of all 7 companies researched — the single methodology phrase found across both pages fetched is "quantitative methodologies" (Political Risk Advisory page), completely unelaborated. No named framework, no confidence language, no source discussion, no reliability grading. Credibility is carried entirely by access-to-named-expert framing rather than by any disclosed process.
- **Psychological pitch:** The purest "bespoke human expertise + exclusive access" pitch of all 7 companies researched — "What distinguishes Eurasia Group advisory is the unfettered access our clients have to analysts—the world's foremost experts on politics and geopolitical risk." Relationship language throughout ("deep, enduring, and profitable" client-analyst relationships). Zero AI-speed framing, zero platform/dashboard framing — a full inversion of the Group 1a pitch.

## Group 1c — Cyber / Adjacent

### Recorded Future
*Site: recordedfuture.com + recordedfuture.com/products/cyber-operations (fetched 2026-09-07)*

- **Trust signals:** Named customers with full attribution (name + title + company) — a pattern shared with Seerist and Dataminr: Erich Harbowy (Security Engineer, Superhuman), Dusan Vignjevic (Head of Threat Intelligence and Threat Hunting, Siemens Energy), John McLeod (CISO, NOV), Robert Antonellis (Director, Threat Management and Intelligence, Avangrid), Jasmina Zito (Head of Threat Intelligence and Red Team, Canva), Masato Kagotani (Security Operations Division GM, Panasonic). Generic institutional claim: "Trusted by industry leaders and governments to defend critical infrastructure worldwide" (no specific government named on the pages fetched). Heavily trademarked proprietary-capability branding used as a credibility device: Intelligence Graph®, Insikt Group®, Collective Insights®, Intelligence Cards®, "Threat Intelligence Company™." **No SOC2/FedRAMP/ISO badge, no award, and — most notably — no visible Mastercard ownership branding** on either page fetched, despite Mastercard's ~$2.6B acquisition of Recorded Future having closed in December 2024 (confirmed via web search this session, see IA-navigation file). The only Mastercard reference found was a co-branded webinar title ("The New Rules of Machine Speed Defense") — framed as a partner/event collaboration, not parent-company backing. Worth flagging as a real, verifiable, and somewhat surprising design/messaging choice: nearly two years into being owned by one of the most recognizable brands in the world, that ownership isn't being used as public-facing trust collateral, at least not on the pages sampled.
- **Tradecraft/methodology:** The most standards-literate of all 7 companies — explicitly names **MITRE ATT&CK** (an actual named, external, industry-standard framework, not a proprietary one) plus concrete rule/detection formats (YARA, Snort, Sigma). Pairs this with a named human-analysis unit, "Insikt Group®," described as adding "expert human analysis, bringing industry, regional, and threat-specific intelligence insights into view." Still no explicit confidence-level taxonomy or source-reliability grading scale in IC-tradecraft terms — this is a cyber-threat-intel framework, not a geopolitical-analytic one — so the rigor language is technical/detection-oriented rather than sourcing/citation-oriented.
- **Psychological pitch:** Speed and autonomy dominate — "machine speed," "machine-speed threats," "31 minutes," "Autonomous Threat Operations," "agentic processing." Layered with preemption framing ("before it matters," "before they happen," "before the pre-attack window closes") and scale/authority ("world's largest threat intelligence company"). Human expertise (Insikt Group) is present but clearly secondary to the AI/automation pitch — the inverse emphasis of Eurasia Group, and more AI-forward even than Seerist or Dataminr in relative weighting.
