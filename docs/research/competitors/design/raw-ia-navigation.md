# Raw Research Notes — Mission 1: Information Architecture & Buyer Journey

Compiled 2026-09-07 for BNOW.NET's pre-redesign competitive teardown. **Methodology note:** live browser navigation (both Claude in Chrome and the linked-desktop browser) was unreachable for the first pass of this research session, so these notes were originally built from automated page-fetch-to-markdown extraction of each competitor's public pages, supplemented by web search where the site was thin, JS-rendered, or ambiguous. This was a real limitation for a couple of competitors whose navigation is heavily client-side rendered (Janes and, partially, Oxford Analytica and Recorded Future). Mid-session, the user installed the Claude in Chrome extension, which unblocked live-browser screenshot capture for Mission 4 — that same pass surfaced the actual rendered nav for Janes and confirmed Recorded Future's, and both are corrected inline below (flagged as "Pass 2 correction," not silently overwritten). Oxford Analytica's nav remains unconfirmed live (only its product page was screenshotted, not the homepage nav), so that gap stands as originally noted. Nav labels and button copy are quoted verbatim in "double quotes" where directly captured; anything paraphrased is marked as such. No strategic synthesis or recommendations below — that comes later.

Sub-questions tracked for every competitor:
1. Primary top-level navigation — organized by Product, by Industry, by Threat Type, by Solution, or some other logic?
2. Primary Call to Action (CTA) — exact button copy and where it leads
3. Product specificity vs. abstract marketing speak — how much of the actual product/mechanism is explained on the public site vs. gestured at

## Group 1a — Structured / API-Deliverable

### Seerist (Control Risks)
*Site: seerist.com + seerist.com/platform (fetched 2026-09-07 via automated page-fetch — live browser screenshot unavailable this session, see visual-branding file for detail)*

- **Top nav (verbatim, in order):** "Platform ▾" | "Solutions ▾" | "Resources ▾" | "Company ▾" | "Book a Demo" | "Log in"
  - **Platform** megamenu: Platform overview, AskAnna, Monitor, PulseAI, Verified Events, DiscoverAI, ExploreAI, Alerts, Content, API, Mobile — organized by *named branded sub-product* (AskAnna, PulseAI, etc.) rather than generic feature labels.
  - **Solutions** megamenu: Why Seerist, Operational Security, Travel Security, Supply Chain Risk, Geopolitical Intelligence, Physical Security, OSINT & Threat Monitoring, Enterprise, Financial Services, Government and Defense — a blend of *threat type* (Travel Security, Supply Chain Risk) and *industry/buyer vertical* (Enterprise, Financial Services, Government and Defense).
  - **Verdict:** hybrid IA — primary nav is Product-led with distinct branded feature names; secondary nav blends Solution/Threat-Type/Industry.
- **Primary CTA:** "Book a Demo" — in the global nav and repeated in the hero. Secondary hero CTA: "Close the Decision Gap →." No self-serve trial CTA anywhere observed; everything routes to a sales conversation. "Log in" is the only non-sales top-level action.
- **Product vs. abstraction:** Leans more concrete than most of the category once you're past the hero. Five distinct named AI sub-products (AskAnna, PulseAI, EventsAI, DiscoverAI, ExploreAI), each described mechanistically — e.g. AskAnna = "Conversational analyst with read-only access to the live world. Ask in plain language; receive answers with citations and timestamps." Quantified specificity throughout: "200+ Control Risks analysts, embedded," "50,000 hours of expert intelligence per year," "39,000 verified global security events this quarter," "6,094 verified war events in Lebanon." Sits alongside abstract category framing ("the operating system for global situational awareness," "Decision-Ready Intelligence"). Net: abstract at the headline/tagline level, concrete one scroll down.
- **Hero headline (verbatim):** "Decisions before disruption." / Subhead: "Risk intelligence that helps you understand what's happening in the world."

### Janes
*Site: janes.com + janes.com/defence-intelligence-products. Original pass fetched 2026-09-07 via automated fetch, which hit a "Janes OSINT" campaign landing variant with no rendered nav; corrected below with a live Claude-in-Chrome screenshot pass, same day.*

- **Nav (Pass 2 correction — verbatim, confirmed via live screenshot):** "PRODUCTS ▾" | "DATASETS ▾" | "COMPANY ▾" | "INSIGHTS ▾" | "PARTNERS ▾" — plus "LOGIN" and a distinct orange pill-button "BOOK A DEMO" set apart from the rest of the nav by color, not just position. This is a clean **Product-led** top nav (Products first, ahead of Company/Insights), consistent with Janes' long-established data-product portfolio — the earlier "unconfirmed, thin JS-rendered nav" gap from Pass 1 is now closed. Supplementary search from Pass 1 (Janes markets named lines like "Janes Defence," "Janes Intelligence," and a dedicated partners/integrations page) still stands as directionally correct context, just not verbatim nav labels.
- **CTA:** "BOOK A DEMO" — confirmed live as an orange pill button, the single highest-contrast element in the entire top nav (everything else is plain text on dark). "LOGIN" is the only other top-level nav action. No self-serve trial CTA observed on either homepage or product page.
- **Product vs. abstraction:** Skews concrete on data-scale claims — "85,000+ Equipment profiles with full specifications," "28,000+ Geolocated installations, including nuclear facilities," "33,000+ Military units with base locations, equipment, and organizational structure," "Over 100 million interconnections." Wrapped in heritage/authority framing that Pass 2 confirms is the live site's actual framing, not a stale campaign artifact: "Decision-Grade Intelligence" (homepage) and "Validated Data. Connected Context. Ready to Brief." (product page) are both real, currently-live headlines, echoing the same "trusted/decision-grade" positioning Pass 1 picked up from the OSINT campaign page.
- **Hero headline (Pass 2 correction, verbatim, live-confirmed):** Homepage: **"Decision-Grade Intelligence."** Product page (janes.com/defence-intelligence-products): **"Validated Data. Connected Context. Ready to Brief."** (Pass 1's "Janes OSINT - The trusted source for Defence Intelligence" / "A Safer World Starts With Trusted Intelligence" headline was real Janes copy, but from a campaign-specific landing page, not the current corporate homepage or product page — both variants may be running concurrently as different entry points, but only the Pass 2 pair above reflects what a visitor lands on going through primary nav.)

### Dataminr
*Site: dataminr.com + dataminr.com/ai-platform (fetched 2026-09-07)*

- **Nav (verbatim top level):** "AI Platform" | "Products" (dropdown: Dataminr for Corporate Security, First Alert, Dataminr for News, Dataminr for Cyber Defense) | "Use Cases" | "Partners" | "Resources" | "Company" — plus utility links "Login" / "Request a demo." Product-led at the top level; the Products dropdown is itself organized by buyer persona/use-case (Corporate Security, Cyber Defense, News) rather than by feature.
- **CTA:** "Request a demo" dominates — repeated at least 3 times (top nav, mid-page, bottom "REQUEST A PERSONALIZED DEMO"). Dense fan-out of secondary "Learn more"/"Read more"/"LEARN MORE" links to product and resource pages. No self-serve trial anywhere.
- **Product vs. abstraction:** Genuinely technical in places — "more than 50 proprietary LLMs," "Multi-Modal Fusion AI for discovery, Generative AI for description, and Agentic AI for context," "detect and respond...across 1M public data sources," "99.5% accuracy" claimed for its auto-regenerating "ReGenAI" briefs. Wrapped in heavy tagline-level abstraction: "deliver the instant clarity needed," "know first, act faster," "eliminates blind spots," "cuts through the noise." More AI-architecture specificity than most competitors seen so far, but almost no mention of a human review/verification step.
- **Hero headline (verbatim):** "AI-Powered Real-Time Event, Threat & Risk Intelligence." AI Platform sub-page headline: "Architecting the Future of Real-Time Intelligence with AI."

### Sayari
*Site: sayari.com (fetched 2026-09-07)*

- **Nav (verbatim, full megamenu):** "Foundation" (Foundation, World Model, Ontology, Superconductor) | "Platform" (Sayari Signal, Graph, Pilot, Sayari Guide) | "Solutions" (compliance use-cases: TPRM, Financial Crime & AML, KYC & Beneficial Ownership, Forced Labor Screening, Export Controls & Sanctions — then industries: Financial Services, Energy, Technology, Manufacturing, Electronics, Aerospace & Defense, Automotive, Consumer Goods — then government use-cases: Export Controls (Government), Trade Enforcement, Threat Finance, Counternarcotics, Critical Infrastructure) | "Resources" | "About" — plus "Login" / "Request a Demo." Distinctive structural choice: **"Foundation" is a top-level nav item that exposes underlying data/AI architecture (World Model, Ontology, "Superconductor") ahead of Platform or Solutions** — they lead with infrastructure credibility before product or industry.
- **CTA:** "Request a Demo" repeated 4+ times (hero, mid-page, final CTA, footer). Secondary CTA "See How It Works." Also runs event-marketing CTAs ("Register Now" / "Watch the Replay" for a recurring "Masterclass" webinar series) — a content-marketing layer not seen elsewhere in Group 1a so far.
- **Product vs. abstraction:** The most concrete of the group. A fully worked example is used as marketing copy: "One Tier-2 supplier – Baikal Industrial Partners, registered Vladivostok 2019 – shares three officers with a sanctioned entity added to OFAC SDN Q3 2024." Headline stat: "1.5B+ entities across 250+ jurisdictions." A side-by-side "Same Query. Two Very Different Answers" module directly contrasts a generic-AI non-answer against Sayari's sourced answer. Abstraction is confined mainly to the tagline layer ("Sovereign AI for Risk Intelligence," "AI THAT EARNS YOUR TRUST").
- **Hero headline (verbatim):** "We Spent A Decade Mapping The World's Shadow Economy." / Subhead: "Now we've built the judgment infrastructure that lets AI navigate it."

## Group 1b — Advisory

### Oxford Analytica
*Site: oxan.com (note: oxfordanalytica.com does not resolve cleanly — the live public brand operates at oxan.com). Fetched 2026-09-07; homepage nav didn't fully render via static fetch, so the core offering page (oxan.com/services/daily-brief/) was fetched directly instead.*

- **Nav:** Not fully captured on the homepage fetch (thin capture, similar to Janes). Via direct URL discovery: the site is organized under "/services/" (e.g. "/services/daily-brief/," "/services/daily-brief-trial-request/") and "/about/people/advisory/" — suggesting a Services-led IA with a separate About/People section, rather than an Industry- or Threat-led structure.
- **CTA:** A fundamentally different buyer-journey model from every Group 1a competitor — the dominant CTA is **"Request a trial" / "Start your free trial today"** (repeated 4× on the core product page) plus a lower-commitment **"Subscribe"** newsletter CTA on the homepage. No "Book/Request a Demo" language anywhere observed — this reads as a media-subscription buying motion (try the briefing, then subscribe) rather than an enterprise-software sales motion (book a call with sales).
- **Product vs. abstraction:** Genuinely concrete about the deliverable's format and cadence — "Full-length articles and in-depth analysis," "Quick-response updates," "Infographics and data visualisations," "Deep dive webinars," "Daily emails and alerts," "Delivered every business day," organized around "What is happening / Why it's happening / What happens next." Unusually specific about *format* compared to the API companies (who are specific about data volume/schema but vaguer about what a deliverable looks like). Wrapped in heritage/impartiality abstraction ("A leader in macroeconomic and geopolitical risk analysis," "robust methodologies and impartial analysis").
- **Hero headline (verbatim, homepage):** "A leader in macroeconomic and geopolitical risk analysis, helping organisations like yours to navigate global shifts and uncertainties." Core product headline (Daily Brief page): "Your passport to global risk mitigation."

### Eurasia Group
*Site: eurasiagroup.net (fetched 2026-09-07)*

- **Nav (verbatim):** "In The News" | "Who We Are ▾" (Our Story, Our Values, Our People, For the Media, Contact Us) | "What We Do ▾" (Political risk advisory, Management consulting, Thought Leadership, Speaking, Events) | "日本語" (Japanese language toggle) | "Careers" | "Client Portal." Organized by **Solution/Service** ("What We Do"), with a distinct, prominent **media/thought-leadership arm** (In The News, GZERO Daily) sitting at the same nav level as the paid advisory service — a structure none of the Group 1a companies have (their "Resources" sections are subordinate content-marketing, not a co-equal nav item).
- **CTA:** Notably soft/editorial rather than sales-first — "Read the Top Risks report," "SUBSCRIBE TO GZERO DAILY," "Get In Touch." "Client Portal" (login-style link) sits in the nav for existing clients. On the actual service page, the only real CTA is "CONTACT US" (a contact form) — no demo, no trial, no self-serve anything.
- **Product vs. abstraction:** The most abstraction-heavy of all 7 competitors on the core offering itself — "Analysts leverage deep country and thematic expertise with quantitative methodologies to forecast fast-moving risks and opportunities" is as specific as it gets; everything else is relationship/access language ("direct interaction with our analysts," "in-person meetings, custom briefings, one-on-one video conferences"). No data schema, no platform, no dashboard, no named AI feature anywhere — the "product" is explicitly a human relationship, not a system.
- **Hero/tagline (verbatim):** "Eurasia Group is made up of highly talented, diverse, and motivated people who are dedicated to defining the business of politics." Service-page headline: "Political risk advisory" / Subhead: "Our ability to combine leading political expertise with timely and actionable insight for clients is at the core of our value."

## Group 1c — Cyber / Adjacent

### Recorded Future
*Site: recordedfuture.com + recordedfuture.com/products/cyber-operations (fetched 2026-09-07). Note: Recorded Future was acquired by Mastercard in a deal that closed December 2024 (~$2.6B, confirmed via web search this session) — the pages fetched here surface almost no visible trace of that ownership (see trust-signals file for detail).*

- **Nav (Pass 2 correction — verbatim, confirmed via live screenshot):** "Why Us" | "Platform" | "Services" | "Insikt Group® Research" | "Resources" | **"Pricing"** — a Product/Solution-led structure, same shape Pass 1 inferred from product-level URLs, but Pass 2 adds one finding Pass 1 could not have caught from a text fetch: **Recorded Future is the only one of all 7 competitors researched with "Pricing" as a visible top-level nav item.** Every other competitor in this research (Group 1a's API vendors included) treats pricing as sales-conversation-gated information, disclosed only after a demo call — Recorded Future alone surfaces it (or at least a path to it) directly in primary nav, a meaningfully different buyer-journey signal worth flagging for BNOW's own IA decision. Pass 1's product-level URL discovery — /products/cyber-operations, /products/digital-risk-protection, /products/third-party-risk, /products/payment-fraud-intelligence — still stands as the underlying **Product-led** structure beneath "Platform," plus the separate media property "The Record" (therecord.media) and its "Cyber Daily™" newsletter linked from the main site.
- **CTA:** "Book demo" / "Request a demo" is dominant (repeated across homepage and product page), alongside a distinctive secondary CTA not seen elsewhere in this research: **"See it in action"**, linking to a self-serve `/platform/demo-center` — an on-demand product tour that needs no live call booked, sitting between "self-serve trial" and "talk to sales." Also unique in this research set: **"Calculate the business value of Recorded Future"** — an ROI-calculator CTA, a distinctly enterprise-procurement-oriented tool not seen on any other competitor's page this pass.
- **Product vs. abstraction:** The most technically dense of all 7 companies researched. Extremely specific, practitioner-legible claims: "MITRE ATT&CK mapping, a TTP matrix dashboard, and dynamic sandbox analysis with live VM interaction," "pre-written YARA, Snort, and Sigma rules," "over 1.5M malware samples detonated daily," "Understand 350+ malware families at the sub-procedure level," "Agentic processing turns vulnerability signals into a production-ready detection signature in as little as 30 minutes." The only competitor in this research to name actual industry-standard technical frameworks (MITRE ATT&CK; YARA/Snort/Sigma rule formats) rather than only proprietary/branded terminology. Abstraction is mostly confined to the top-level tagline: "Know what matters before it matters," "Defend autonomously at machine speed."
- **Hero headline (verbatim):** "Know what matters before it matters." / Subhead: "Defend autonomously at machine speed with the world's largest threat intelligence company." Product-page headline: "Cyber Operations" / Subhead: "Stop drowning in alerts. Start stopping threats."
