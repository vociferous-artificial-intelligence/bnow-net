# O5 — Design-partner program

**Phase:** 5 Surface · **Owner:** OPERATOR · **Depends on:** A1 (who), A16 (evidence memo,
terms), O2 (packaging: what founding terms are) · **Feeds:** O6, A18 wave 1, A19 · **Time:**
outreach ~10 hours over 3–4 weeks; then ~2 hours/week running it.

## Why this cannot be delegated

Design partners are the first customers, the first testimonials, and the first honest
feedback loop. They come from relationships and from the O1 pool; an agent can draft the
message and the terms, but a person makes the ask and keeps the promise.

## Preparation

1. **Candidates**: O1 reports where §9 says yes to a design-partner conversation, ranked by
   segment fit and access; plus any pre-existing contacts in the outreach roster. Aim to ask
   15 to land 5–10.
2. **Terms (from O2/A16)**: what they get (access, founding pricing or free period, direct
   line, influence on roadmap, early features), what they give (weekly 20-minute feedback
   call for n weeks, use in real work, a quotable line and logo *when they are satisfied*,
   not before), duration, exit. One page, plain language. No exclusivity unless deliberate.
3. **Onboarding kit**: the A16 evidence memo, sign-in instructions, a 15-minute walkthrough
   you can give live, the feedback form (below).
4. **Cadence**: a fixed weekly slot per partner or a shared office hour; a shared channel or
   email thread per partner.

## How to run it

Ask personally, one message each, referencing what they told you in O1. Onboard each partner
live — watch them do the first task; that is your first usability data. Keep a **partner log**
(one file) with: date, partner id, what they did, what they said (verbatim), what broke, what
they asked for, what you promised, status of each promise. Review it weekly; a promise not
kept within two weeks is the most common way programs die.

Feedback call structure (20 min): what did you use it for this week → what got in the way →
what did you want that wasn't there → would you miss it if it went away tomorrow, why →
anything you'd tell a peer.

Ask for the quotable line and logo only after they volunteer satisfaction, and get written
consent for use.
