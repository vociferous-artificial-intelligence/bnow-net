# O3 — Message test

**Phase:** 3 Positioning · **Owner:** OPERATOR · **Depends on:** O2 (variants chosen),
A10 (variants), A11 (stack) · **Feeds:** A12, A15, A16 · **Time:** ~8 hours over one week
for 5–8 short sessions.

## Why this cannot be delegated

The only way to know whether a position line is understood, believed and seen as different
is to put it in front of the people it is for and watch. Fifteen minutes each, five to eight
people, no product demo. This is the cheapest test in the whole process and the one most
often skipped.

## Preparation

1. From `O2`, the 2–3 position variants (each ≤ 40 words) and the top three proof points.
   Put each variant on its own plain page — headline, one sub-line, the proofs as three
   short lines — with no logo, no colour, identical layout. Label them A, B, C.
2. Recruit 5–8 people from the O1 pool who agreed to follow-up (§9 of their reports),
   ideally spanning the beachhead and one other segment. Add 1–2 people who have never heard
   of the product.
3. Randomise the order you show variants per person.

## How to run a 15-minute session

Show one variant for 10 seconds, then hide it. Ask, and write down verbatim:

1. **Comprehension** — "What is this? What does it do? Who is it for?" (Score 0–2: wrong /
   partly / right.)
2. **Recall** — "What's the one thing you remember?" (Which proof stuck.)
3. **Credibility** — "What here do you doubt? What would you need to see to believe it?"
   (Score 0–2.)
4. **Differentiation** — "How is this different from [the vendor they named in O1]?"
   (Score 0–2: same / vague / specific.)
5. **Language** — "Say it back in your own words." (Capture — this is copy.)

Repeat for each variant. Then show all together: "Which would you click? Which would you
forward to your boss? Which sounds like a company you'd trust with a decision?" Ask why.

Do not explain, defend or demo. If they misread it, that is the finding.

list has evidence actions attached.
