# O2 — Positioning & packaging decision memo

**Phase:** 3 Positioning · **Owner:** OPERATOR · **Depends on:** A1, A9, A10, A11 (A3 for
pricing) · **Feeds:** O3, A12, A13, A15, A16, A18 and the project's decision log · **Time:**
one sitting of 2–3 hours after reading the four inputs (allow a day for reading).

## Why this cannot be delegated

Agents framed the options and gathered the evidence. Choosing the buyer, the promise, the
name and the price model is the founder's job, and not choosing is the most expensive
option: every downstream step (identity, copy, pricing page, launch) blocks on it. In the
project that prompted this playbook two such decisions sat open for nine weeks.

## Preparation

Read, in this order: `A1` (what buyers said), `A9` (matrix, maps, self-assessment,
assumption test), `A10` §2–4 and §7 (variants, proofs, anti-positioning, decisions list),
`A11` §5–6 and §9 (stack, segment architecture, undecided), `A3` summary (price evidence).
Have the open-task / decision log open.

## How to run it

Answer each question below in writing, in the memo. For each: the choice, the options you
rejected, the evidence you relied on (cite A1 report ids and A9/A10 sections), what would
change your mind, and the review date. A decision made with "medium" confidence and a
review date beats an open question.

If you cannot decide a question, write *why* — the specific missing evidence — and the step
that will produce it (usually more O1 interviews or O3). Then decide the rest; do not let one
open item hold the others.

## Questions the memo must answer

1. **Category term** — what we call the category, in the buyer's words (A1 §6 counts).
2. **Beachhead segment** — which ICP first, with the A1 evidence (pain strength, ability to
   pay, access). Which segments are explicitly *not* now, and which are GATED on what.
3. **Position line** — which of A10's three variants goes to O3 for testing, and any edit.
   (Do not pick the final one yet; O3 does that with buyers.)
4. **Proof points** — the three to five we lead with, each SAYABLE today. Anything ROADMAP
   we allow ourselves to say, with its date.
5. **The gating product decision(s)** — whatever product question blocks a claim (e.g.
   automated-only vs human-reviewed tier; rights to present a data source). Decide, or
   set a dated deadline and what happens by default.
6. **Name** — keep or change; the reasoning; if change, the timing and the transition.
7. **Packaging model** — per-seat / per-org / usage / hybrid; tiers; whether pricing is
   published (A3 evidence on category norms and A11 on transparency as a message). If
   withheld, the public wording and the revisit date.
8. **Price points** (if published) — the numbers and the basis (A3 comparators, A1 WTP
   signals, unit economics).
9. **Competitive naming** — whether copy may name competitors (default: no).
10. **Voice and anti-positioning** — ratify A10 §4–5 or list edits.
11. **Narrative stack** — ratify A11 §5 order or reorder, with reasons.
