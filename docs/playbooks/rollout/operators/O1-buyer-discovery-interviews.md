# O1 — Buyer discovery interviews

**Phase:** 1 Discovery · **Owner:** OPERATOR · **Depends on:** the ICP list and, if the
project has one, the register of what may be claimed in outreach · **Feeds:** A1, and through
it everything in Phases 3–6 · **Time:** ~25 hours over 2–3 weeks for 8–12 interviews
(outreach 6h, interviews 8–10h, reports 6h).

## Why this cannot be delegated

An agent can research what competitors say; only a human can find out what a buyer needs, in
their words, and hear what they *don't* say. Every later step cites these reports. Without
them, positioning, copy and design are guesses — well-organised guesses, but guesses.

## Minimum

Eight completed interviews across at least two segments, with at least three in the
segment the strategy docs call the beachhead. Twelve is better. Stop when the last three
interviews taught you nothing new (saturation) and you have the minimum.

## Preparation (half a day)

1. **Who.** From the ICP list, name the *role* that feels the pain and the *role* that owns
   budget; you want both, in roughly 2:1. Build a list of 30–40 names: personal network,
   former colleagues, LinkedIn 2nd-degree, conference speaker lists, authors of relevant
   posts, people who have complained publicly about incumbents. Track in a sheet: name,
   org type, role, segment, source, status.
2. **Ask.** A three-sentence message: who you are, that you are researching how people in
   their role do X (not selling), and a 30-minute request. Offer to share what you learn.
   Do not pitch the product in the ask. Follow the project's claims rules if it has them.
3. **Consent.** Decide how you record (notes only, or recording with explicit consent at
   the start). Say how you will use it ("anonymised, internal").
4. **Guide.** Use the script below; adapt segment-specific words. Print it.

## How to run a 30–40 minute interview

Rule one: **discovery first, concept second, never mixed.** The first 20–25 minutes are
about *their* work with no mention of your product. Only in the last 10 do you show or
describe the concept, and the report keeps the two apart.

Rule two: ask about the past and the present, never the hypothetical future. "Tell me about
the last time…" beats "Would you use…".

Rule three: shut up. Silence after an answer produces the second, truer answer.

### Script

**Opening (2 min).** Thanks, purpose, consent, "no right answers, I'm here to learn how this
actually works for you."

**Their job (8 min).**
- Walk me through what you were doing yesterday / the last time [the situation] came up.
- What were you trying to get to? Who was waiting on you?
- What did you use? (Tools, sources, people.) What do those cost — money, time, hassle?
- What's the worst part of that? When did it last go wrong? What happened?

**Evaluation and buying (8 min).**
- Last time you brought in a new tool or source for this — how did that start? Who
  decided? Who had to approve (security, procurement, legal)? How long did it take?
- How did you judge the options? What made you trust one over another?
- What do you pay now for this kind of thing, roughly? What would make that feel expensive
  / cheap?
- What have you tried and dropped? Why?

**Their words (4 min).**
- If you were describing this category to a new colleague, what would you call it?
- Which vendors come to mind? What's the one-line reputation of each?
- What would have to be true for you to switch from what you use now?

**Concept (10 min — only now).** Show the product or a one-paragraph description. Then:
- What do you think this is? (Comprehension — record their words.)
- What would you do with it first?
- What would make you not trust it?
- Who else in your org would care? Who would object?
- If it did what it says, what would it be worth to you — compared to what?

**Close (2 min).** Who else should I talk to? May I come back to test wording with you in a
few weeks? Thank them; send the promised summary later.

## The report you must file — one per interview

Path: `<<OUT>>/1-discovery/O1-<###>-<segment>-<date>.md`. Write it within 24 hours; memory
decays fast. Verbatims in quotation marks are the most valuable thing in the file — write
down their exact words, not your paraphrase.

```
# O1-<###> — <segment> — <date>

**Step:** O1 · **Phase:** 1 Discovery · **Owner:** OPERATOR · **Status:** REPORT
**Consumed:** ICP list; interview guide v<n>
**Feeds:** A1
**Method:** <call/in person, duration, recorded Y/N with consent, interviewer>
**Confidence:** <HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW — e.g. LOW if they were guarded or off-segment>

## Subject (private — not copied into synthesis)
Name · organisation · role · seniority · region · how sourced · follow-up permission Y/N

## Profile (anonymised — this is what A1 uses)
Segment · org type and size band · role type (user / budget owner / both) · region

## 1. Job and context
What they were trying to accomplish, in their words. The last concrete episode.

## 2. Current solution and its cost
Tools, sources, people. Money (numbers if given), time, risk. "…" verbatims.

## 3. Pains (ranked as they ranked them, unprompted first)
- "…"
- "…"

## 4. Evaluation and buying
How they chose last time; who decided; approvals; timeline; what built trust.

## 5. Willingness to pay
What they pay now; what they compared it to; any number they said. Mark "not asked" if so.

## 6. Their language
Category name they used · vendors named and one-line reputations · phrases for the problem
and the outcome (verbatim).

## 7. Concept reaction (kept separate)
What they thought it was · first use · trust objections · who else cares / objects · value
comparison. Verbatims.

## 8. Disconfirming evidence
Anything that contradicts our assumptions (ICP, wedge, price, category). Be honest here.

## 9. Follow-ups
Referrals given · agreed to message test (O3) Y/N · agreed to design-partner conversation
(O5) Y/N · what to send them.

## 10. Interviewer notes
What surprised you. What you'd ask differently next time.
```

## Done when

Eight or more reports exist in this form; the profile section is anonymised; sections 3, 6,
7 and 8 are filled in every report (a blank §8 is a red flag — ask yourself whether you
were selling instead of listening); the tracking sheet shows the pipeline for the next round.

## Common failures

Pitching in the first 20 minutes. Asking "would you pay for…". Interviewing only friends.
Only interviewing users and never budget owners. Paraphrasing instead of quoting. Filing the
report three days later.
