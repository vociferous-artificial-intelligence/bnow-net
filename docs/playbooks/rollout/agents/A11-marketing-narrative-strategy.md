# A11 — Marketing narrative strategy draft

**Phase:** 3 Positioning · **Owner:** AGENT → OPERATOR (O2 ratifies the stack) · **Depends
on:** A1, A9, A10 · **Feeds:** O2, O3, A15, A16, A18, A19 · **Budget:** read-only.

(Prepend `COMMON.md`. Fill placeholders.)

## Objective

Inventory the stories competitors sell, judge which hold up, and propose the narrative stack
and per-segment message architecture `<<PROJECT>>` will use — plus the channel plan and the
KPIs by which it will be judged. Options and evidence for the operator; no copy yet.

## Inputs

- `A1` (what buyers say moves them; their words), `A9` (messaging-hierarchy comparison,
  unclaimed positions), `A10` (position variants, proof points, anti-positioning),
  `A3` (price transparency evidence), `A6` (trust signals), `<<STRATEGY_DOCS>>` (segments,
  channels already tried, budget).

## Method

1. **Narrative inventory.** Every distinct selling story in the competitor set (e.g. speed,
   human-analyst hours, anti-AI purity, AI architecture, borrowed authority, heritage,
   exclusive access, analyst relief, explainability, personal accountability). For each: who
   runs it (verbatim, A5/A6 refs), the buyer need it targets (A1 refs), how well it holds up
   (what evidence the vendor shows), and its weak point.
2. **Narratives nobody runs** — from A9 §4 — and why that might be.
3. **Scoring.** Table: narrative · credibility (shown vs asserted) · resonance with our
   buyers (A1 counts) · defensibility for us (SAYABLE proof?) · risk. Short judgements.
4. **Adopt / Improve / Counter.** Three lists. *Adopt*: narratives we can run better with
   proof. *Improve*: category narratives we can make concrete. *Counter*: competitor claims
   we can reframe without naming the vendor (give the reframe sentence). Rule: no
   comparative marketing against named vendors unless the operator decides otherwise.
5. **Narrative stack** — ranked list of 4–6 messages in order of emphasis, each with its
   proof, tag (SAYABLE/ROADMAP/GATED), and the buyer objection it answers.
6. **Message architecture by segment** — for each ICP in `<<ICP_LIST>>`: the decisive
   question they ask (A1), lead message, proof, CTA, and what *not* to say to them. Mark
   segments GATED where the proof is.
7. **Channel plan** — for each channel (product surfaces as marketing, content/brief,
   partner/validator outreach, communities and events, search, social, paid, PR): fit for our
   buyers (A1 evidence of where they look), cost, owner, cadence, and the first three
   concrete pieces. Say which channels we will *not* use and why.
8. **KPIs** — proposed funnel metrics with targets for 90 days after launch and where each
   number will come from (A17 instruments them).
9. **Undecided** — what O2 must settle before A15 writes copy.

## Output — `<<OUT>>/3-positioning/A11-narrative-strategy-<<DATE>>.md`

COMMON header (Status: SYNTHESIS; `PROVISIONAL` if no A1). Sections 1–9 · Sources.

## Done when

Every narrative in the inventory cites a vendor and a raw section; the stack has ≤ 6
messages each with a tag and proof; every ICP has an architecture row; the channel plan
names owners and first pieces; KPIs have sources.

## Do not

- Do not write final copy or taglines; give message intents and one example line at most.
- Do not propose a channel our buyers gave no evidence of using without labelling it a bet.
